## Current position

MentoMate's persistent memory consists of (i) a per-learner learning profile (`learning_profiles` table: interests, strengths, struggles, communication notes, learning style), (ii) derived `memory_facts` rows with vector embeddings, (iii) per-session summaries, and (iv) per-session transcript embeddings with a stored text copy. The profile fields are populated by an LLM transcript-analysis step that emits free-text signals — including verbatim `topic` strings derived from the child's conversation — which are stored as-is after gating (`apps/api/src/services/learner-profile.ts:401-408`, `topic: signal.topic.trim()`). The Article 9 exposure is therefore real and foreseeable: free text derived from a child's free conversation enters persistent storage.

A server-side suppression mechanism **does exist today** for the highest-risk category. Every write to the profile fields and to `memory_facts` passes through the persisted-learning-text safety gate (WI-2628, MMT-ADR-0036: `apps/api/src/services/learning-text-safety/scan.ts`, wired into the write transaction at `apps/api/src/services/learner-profile.ts:1692-1809`). The gate deterministically blocks person-attributed clinical/diagnostic text (block reasons `person_attribution`, `diagnostic_inference`, fail-closed `unclear`; `scan.ts:82-85`), is enforced in the database transaction with a coverage check (not merely instructed to the LLM), fails closed on ambiguity for LLM-authored text, and blocked items feed an operator digest (`apps/api/src/services/blocked-safety-digest.ts`). A retroactive remediation path for already-persisted rows also exists (WI-2753: `apps/api/src/services/learning-text-safety/persisted-remediation-memory.ts`). Separately, a deterministic special-category redaction filter runs at every learner-data egress boundary — all LLM provider calls (`apps/api/src/services/llm/router.ts:1840, 2378`) and the Voyage embedding call (`apps/api/src/services/embeddings.ts:170`) — replacing first-person special-category disclosures with `[sensitive personal information removed]` (`apps/api/src/services/learner-egress-filter.ts:4-13, 54-81`).

The honest limits of that position are threefold. First, the write-gate's protected-lexeme corpus covers **health/disability/diagnostic terms only** — it does not cover religion, sexual orientation, ethnicity, or political opinions, and only its English list is expert-reviewed; the other nine language corpora are model-generated and explicitly marked as requiring native-speaker review before being relied on as a compliance control (`apps/api/src/services/learning-text-safety/corpus.ts:11-16, 86`). Second, there is **no tripwire-to-memory firewall at the transcript level**: a safeguarding disclosure that trips the deterministic safety tripwire is still persisted as an ordinary transcript event and is not excluded from the downstream analysis input or the stored transcript-embedding text (details in §Suppression and exclusion analysis). Third, the stored `session_embeddings.content` column retains the raw, unfiltered conversation text (the egress filter applies only to the payload sent to Voyage), bounded by the 30-day transcript purge. These are recorded as findings, not papered over.

Operationally, persistent memory collection is disabled by default and consent-gated in code: `memory_consent_status` defaults to `'pending'` and `memory_collection_enabled` defaults to `false` (`packages/database/src/schema/learning-profiles.ts:36-46`), and both the analysis-transmission step and the profile write refuse unless consent is `'granted'` and collection is enabled (`apps/api/src/inngest/functions/session-completed.ts:1722-1748`; `apps/api/src/services/learner-profile.ts:1649-1662, 1742-1747`). This is consistent with the interim condition that memory and profiling stay disabled pre-approval. ## Article 9 position (memory-specific)

**Categories that can incidentally arise.** In a free-text mentoring conversation with a child, all Article 9 categories are foreseeable in incidental disclosure or inference: health and disability (including learning-disability and mental-health inferences — the category the product is structurally most likely to *generate*, since the analysis step characterises how a learner learns), religious or philosophical beliefs, sexual orientation and sex life, racial or ethnic origin, political opinions. Data concerning a child's exposure to abuse (a safeguarding disclosure) is not itself an enumerated Art 9 category but is treated in this package as safeguarding-sensitive content of at least equivalent gravity; it can also carry Art 9 content (e.g. health consequences).

**What the current position document says.** `docs/compliance/art9-special-category-position.md` (status: "Proposed controller position for DPO/privacy-counsel advice", as of 2026-07-24) states that MentoMate does not ask for or intend to use special-category data; acknowledges that incidental disclosure, inference, transmission and temporary storage "are … foreseeable and must not be described categorically as impossible"; and proposes safeguards including to "exclude sensitive characterisations from persistent learning memory, profiling, assessment, and guardian-facing summaries" and to "handle genuine crisis content through a separate safeguarding procedure". It draws the distinction between *discussing* a sensitive topic and creating a *sensitive characterisation of the learner* ("this learner is likely dyslexic" being outside product purpose). The historical "no Article 9 processing" decision is superseded and retained only as provenance (`docs/compliance/art9-special-category-position.md`, final paragraph).

**The exclude-from-memory safeguard is partially implemented.** For the health/disability/diagnostic category, the proposed exclusion is implemented as server-side enforcement (the persisted-learning-text gate, evidence below). For the remaining Art 9 categories it is implemented only at the egress boundary (redaction before provider transmission), not at the storage boundary. The position document's exclusion claim should therefore be presented to the DPO as *implemented for diagnostic characterisations; partial for other categories* — see Gaps 1 and 2.

**Art 9(2) condition — open.** The position document expressly asks the DPO to determine (1) whether the intended-use vs incidental-disclosure distinction is sound, (2) whether foreseeable incidental handling constitutes Article 9 processing at all, and (3) "which Article 9 condition, if any, is required" (`art9-special-category-position.md`, "Advice required"). No Art 9(2) condition has been adopted by the controller. No condition is asserted in this package. If the DPO concludes a condition is required for the incidental/transient handling, the realistic candidates on the current design are Art 9(2)(a) (explicit consent — problematic for children and for data the user did not set out to provide) or a minimisation-based position that the processing is limited, unintended, suppressed at the persistence boundary, and time-bounded (30-day raw-content purge, §below); the choice is precisely the advice requested. [GAP: Art 9(2) condition determination — this is the DPO's deliverable under Action 5; this package supplies the factual substrate.]

## Suppression and exclusion analysis

This section gives the write-path account requested by the DPO: what enters persistent memory, from where, and what filtering exists at each stage. Throughout, **prompt-level instruction** (asking an LLM to behave) is distinguished from **server-side enforcement** (deterministic code that decides what persists regardless of LLM behaviour). Only the latter is claimed as a control.

**Stage 0 — capture.** Every learner message and mentor reply in a session is persisted as a `session_events` row (`apps/api/src/services/session/session-exchange.ts:4005-4021`). There is no content-based filtering at capture; the transcript is the raw record of the conversation. Raw transcripts are purged on a 30-day clock (evidence row 14; owned by the Action 6 section).

**Stage 0a — the safety tripwire (safeguarding path).** Before any LLM call, `detectCatastrophicSafetyTrigger` (`apps/api/src/services/safety-tripwire.ts:180-190`) screens the input (and OCR-extracted image text, `exchanges.ts:1912-1934`) for three catastrophic categories: self-harm method-seeking/intent, sexual content involving a minor, and first-person abuse disclosure. On a hit the exchange short-circuits to a deterministic crisis-redirect reply (helpline, trusted adult; `safety-tripwire.ts:221-253`) and a safety event is emitted. The safety event pipeline is content-free by design: log line, Sentry operator alarm, and telemetry event carry correlation id, flow, session/profile ids only — "Metadata + profileId-scoped pointers ONLY — no disclosure content" (`exchanges.ts:100-145`). The safety event itself therefore contributes nothing sensitive to persistent storage. Additionally, the conversational prompt binds a `crisis_redirect` envelope signal for model-detected safeguarding concerns (`apps/api/src/services/exchange-prompts.ts:378, 800-808`) — that signal is observational telemetry, likewise content-free at emission.

**The tripwire now creates a memory exclusion (WI-3140).** Detection stamps the session-level marker `learning_sessions.safety_flagged_at`, and the session-completion pipeline skips both memory-producing steps when it is set: transcript analysis returns before the event read (`session-completed.ts:1750-1774`, ahead of `:1776`) and the embedding step returns before anything reaches Voyage or `session_embeddings` (`:2107-2128`). Exclusion of safeguarding content from persistent memory therefore no longer rests only on the downstream gates (Stages 2-3, which target diagnostic characterisations): there is now a deterministic firewall at the safeguarding-detection point. What the firewall does NOT do is un-capture the conversation — the learner's disclosing message remains a `session_events` row until the 30-day purge. See Gap 3 for the full write/enforcement map and the regression guard.

**Stage 1 — transcript analysis (LLM, prompt level).** At session completion, if and only if memory consent is `'granted'`, collection is enabled, and GDPR/LLM-disclosure consent is intact (`session-completed.ts:1722-1748` — the gate deliberately sits before transmission, not just before the write), the transcript is sent for analysis. Egress passes the special-category redaction filter at the router (`router.ts:1840, 2378`; filter at `learner-egress-filter.ts:54-81`): first-person disclosure cues combined with special-category terms (health, religion, sexuality, ethnicity, political, genetic/biometric) are replaced with `[sensitive personal information removed]`, with an educational-context exemption so schoolwork about these topics is not destroyed. This is server-side, deterministic — but it is an **egress/transmission minimisation** (English-pattern-based), not a storage control. The analysis prompt (`learner-profile.ts:77-124`) then instructs conservative extraction of interests/strengths/struggles/communication notes and exclusion of `{suppressedTopics}`. The prompt contains no instruction regarding sensitive categories (Gap 4); and even if it did, a prompt instruction is not enforcement and is claimed as nothing more than defence-in-depth.

**Stage 2 — the profile/memory write (server-side enforcement — the operative control).** `applyAnalysis` (`learner-profile.ts:1614-1830`) is the sole write path from analysis output to persistent memory. It (a) re-checks GDPR consent outside and inside the transaction (`:1659-1662, 1731-1740`) and the memory-consent/collection gate (`:1742-1747`); (b) merges the LLM signals honouring the parent/learner `suppressed_inferences` list (evidence row 13); and (c) passes **every candidate string** — profile fields, struggle notifications, and the derived `memory_facts` row texts — through the persisted-learning-text gate before anything is written (`:1692-1722` evaluation; `:1769-1789` in-transaction coverage checks; `:1773-1809` only the sanitised projection and gated fact rows are persisted). The gate itself (`scan.ts`) deterministically blocks person-attributed clinical text (`person_attribution`, `diagnostic_inference`), routes user-authored ambiguity to an independent judge whose pool excludes the producing vendor, and **fails closed** (`block`, reason `unclear`) for LLM-authored text with missing producer identity and for migration ambiguity. Blocks are counted into an operator digest without content (`blocked-safety-digest.ts`). This is the concrete implementation of the position document's "exclude sensitive characterisations from persistent learning memory" — **scoped to health/disability/diagnostic characterisations** (the corpus, evidence row 5), with expert-reviewed coverage in English only (Gaps 1-2).

**Stage 3 — embeddings.** Two distinct stores. (i) `memory_facts.embedding`: vectors are generated only for fact rows that survived the Stage 2 gate (`session-completed.ts:151-178` embeds existing `memory_facts` rows), and the Voyage payload passes the egress filter (`embeddings.ts:159-183`). (ii) `session_embeddings`: the raw session transcript (up to 8,000 chars of user + mentor turns) is embedded per session; the Voyage payload is egress-filtered (`embeddings.ts:170`), but the row **stores the pre-filter raw text** in its `content` column (`embeddings.ts:285-301`; `packages/database/src/schema/embeddings.ts:31`) and the step is gated on GDPR consent but **not** on memory consent (`session-completed.ts:2058-2092`) — Gaps 5-6. At the 30-day transcript purge the raw-content embedding row is replaced by a summary-derived one (retention draft §3.2), which time-bounds but does not eliminate the exposure.

**Stage 4 — read-back into prompts.** Memory is injected into future mentor prompts only when consent is `'granted'` and injection is enabled (`buildMemoryBlock`, `learner-profile.ts:848-853`), so a suppression at Stage 2 also suppresses all downstream surfacing to the child and (via the gated struggle notifications, evidence row 17) to guardians.

**Summary of the account.** Server-side enforcement exists at the persistence boundary for diagnostic/clinical characterisations (transaction-enforced, fail-closed, observable, with retroactive remediation), plus deterministic special-category redaction at every external-transmission boundary, plus consent gating that keeps the entire collection path off by default. What does not yet exist: content-based exclusion at the storage boundary for the non-health Art 9 categories; a safeguarding-to-memory firewall; non-English reviewed corpora; and memory-consent gating plus filtered storage on the session-embedding path.

## Gaps and proposed remediation

Numbered findings; "proposed" throughout — none of these remedies is implemented unless cited above. Tracking IDs are given where a repo artifact names one; otherwise marked for capture.

1. **Write-gate corpus covers health/disability only.** The Stage 2 gate's protected-lexeme corpus (`corpus.ts`) contains no religion, sexual-orientation, ethnicity, or political-opinion lexemes, so a person-attributed statement in those categories (e.g. a stored interest or communication note referencing the learner's religion) would pass the storage gate; the only current defence is the LLM's extraction conservatism plus the egress redaction on later transmissions. *Proposed remedy:* extend the corpus and attribution grammars to the remaining Art 9 categories, or (narrower, likely sufficient) extend the gate's blocklist semantics: block person-attribution constructions over a broadened Art 9 term set at `fieldKind: 'learner_profile_field'` and `'memory_fact'`. Requires a WI — none found for this specific extension. [GAP: no tracking ID — capture as a Cosmo WI in the memory-unlock workstream.]
2. **Nine of ten language corpora are unreviewed.** Marked `'model-generated'` and explicitly "REQUIRES native-speaker review before it can be relied on as a compliance control" (`corpus.ts:11-16`). Until reviewed, the gate's Art 9 claim is evidence-grade for English conversations only. *Proposed remedy:* commission native-speaker review of the cs/es/fr/de/it/pt/pl/ja/nb corpora before memory is enabled for those conversation languages, or restrict memory collection to English-language conversations at unlock. [GAP: no tracking ID found — capture.]
3. **Tripwire-to-memory firewall (was the headline finding) — CLOSED, WI-3140.** *Was:* a first-person abuse disclosure or self-harm statement that fired the tripwire was still included in the analysis input sent to the LLM (redacted only insofar as the English egress regexes catch it) and still stored verbatim in `session_embeddings.content`; exclusion from *profile* memory depended on the analysis LLM not emitting it as a signal and the Stage 2 gate catching clinical attributions — neither a safeguarding-specific control. *Now:* a session-level, server-side, deterministic firewall. **Marker:** `learning_sessions.safety_flagged_at` (`packages/database/src/schema/sessions.ts:159-172`; migration `apps/api/drizzle/0169_crazy_wildside.sql`) — a nullable timestamp and nothing else, content-free by design, so recording *that* a disclosure happened never re-persists *what* was said (same metadata-only constraint as `emitCrisisRedirectEvent`, ruling se-032). **Write sites** (`apps/api/src/services/safety-flag.ts` → `flagSessionSafety`, `profileId`-scoped per PRIN-04), called from `apps/api/src/services/session/session-exchange.ts:4405-4411` (non-streaming) and `:4939-4947` (streaming), covering both detector families: the deterministic tripwire (text, image-OCR screen, and the unscreenable-image fail-safe, surfaced as `ExchangeResult.safetyFlagged` / `ExchangeStreamResult.safetyFlagged` in `apps/api/src/services/exchanges.ts`) and the model-layer `signals.crisis_redirect` envelope signal. **Enforcement sites** — a fresh in-step read of the marker skips the entire step: `analyze-learner-profile` before the transcript is read or sent to the LLM (`apps/api/src/inngest/functions/session-completed.ts:1750-1774`) and `generate-embeddings` before anything reaches Voyage or the index (`:2107-2128`); each emits a structured `safety.*_skipped` log line. **Regression guard:** `apps/api/src/inngest/functions/session-completed-safety-firewall.integration.test.ts` — a flagged session run through the real pipeline against a real database yields zero profile-memory writes, zero `memory_facts` rows and zero `session_embeddings` rows, while an unflagged control still produces both; a companion negative-path case proves the marker cannot be written across profiles. Detector coverage is pinned in `apps/api/src/services/exchanges.safety-flag.test.ts`. This evidences the position document's "handle genuine crisis content through a separate safeguarding procedure" as applied to memory. *Still open (deliberately out of WI-3140 scope):* the flagged session's raw transcript remains in `session_events` on the ordinary 30-day purge clock — shortening the retention clock for flagged sessions was the optional layer (b) and is not implemented.
