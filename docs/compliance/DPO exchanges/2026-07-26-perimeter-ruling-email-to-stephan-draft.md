Dear Stephan,

Following your Interim DPIA Advice Record, we have now made the management decision on the launch perimeter — the open point behind confirmation M3. The two attached documents record it; a summary and two specific questions for you are below. The full indexed response to the remaining confirmations and action items will follow separately.

**The ruling (attachment 1).** The launch perimeter is an allowlist with two admission routes:

- **Route 1 — EEA:** countries whose verified Article 8 self-consent threshold is 13, per the country register we sent previously (attachment 3), with its launch-day re-verification.
- **Route 2 — non-EEA:** a jurisdiction is admitted only via a documented, dated screen record concluding that (a) our 13+ age floor satisfies local child-consent requirements, (b) no local representative, registration, or licensing duties apply at our scale, (c) no local child-safety, AI, or platform regime imposes material obligations beyond our GDPR-level controls (with thresholds and monitoring triggers recorded), and (d) management signs a risk acceptance naming the revisit triggers.

Everything not admitted is unavailable, enforced by store distribution configuration and in-app residence gating. Consequences: the **United States is in** (first Route 2 screen, attachment 2); the **UK, Poland, and Switzerland are out at launch** and become expansion candidates (UK: Art 27 representative + Children's Code/OSA; Poland and other higher-threshold EEA: pending our guardian-authorisation flow; Switzerland: representative requirement).

**Proposed M3 response.** We intend to enter the following into the Advice Record as our M3 confirmation — please tell us if you want it adjusted:

> Confirmed with revision: the launch perimeter consists of (i) EEA countries whose launch-day verified Article 8 self-consent threshold is 13, per our maintained country register, and (ii) non-EEA jurisdictions individually cleared by a documented admission screen (procedure attached) confirming that our 13+ age floor satisfies local child-consent requirements, that no local representative or registration duties apply at our scale, and that no material local regime exceeds our GDPR-level controls. The United States is the first jurisdiction cleared under (ii) (screen record attached). Uncertain, higher-threshold, and unscreened countries remain unavailable, enforced by store distribution configuration and in-app residence gating.

**Question 1 — Article 8 law applicable to non-EEA minors.** Since ZWIZZLY AS is established in Norway, GDPR applies to all our processing regardless of user location. For minors resident outside the EEA (e.g. a 13-year-old in the US), our working position is that the Norwegian Article 8 implementation — threshold 13 — applies as the law of the controller's establishment, so such a user may self-consent. The US screen is expressly conditional on your concurrence with this reading. Do you concur, or would you analyse it differently?

**Question 2 — scope of your DPO engagement.** GDPR follows us as the controller, so we understand your statutory DPO role covers all our processing worldwide — we are not proposing to carve users out by geography. What we do propose is clarifying that your advisory scope is GDPR/Norwegian/EEA law, and that compliance with non-EEA regimes (US federal and state law, UK law, etc.) remains our responsibility, supported by the screen procedure above. Concretely, in the Services Agreement under "DPO tasks — Information and advice", we would amend:

> "…obligations under the GDPR, the Norwegian Personal Data Act and other applicable data-protection requirements"

to:

> "…obligations under the GDPR, the Norwegian Personal Data Act and other applicable EEA/Norwegian data-protection requirements. Compliance with non-EEA legal regimes (including US federal and state law and UK law) remains the Company's responsibility and is outside the statutory DPO scope, without prejudice to the GDPR's application to all of the Company's processing regardless of user location."

Is this consistent with your intended scope, and acceptable as an amendment? If so, we are otherwise ready to sign both the Services Agreement and the Formal Designation as drafted (effective date pending the dpo@ mailbox setup per our separate email).

One US item remains open on our side independent of your review: the state App Store Accountability Acts (Texas/Utah/Louisiana — app-store age verification with developer-side signal handling, no size thresholds). It is tracked internally and will be resolved before we sign the screen's risk acceptance; the screen record marks it as an open condition.

The US screen's risk-acceptance signature and the M1–M7 entries will be finalised once we have your view on the two questions.
