# Received: DPO answers to Q1–Q6 (2026-07-31)

Dear Zuzana,

Thank you for your detailed response and for incorporating my recommendations. I am pleased that we are now close to completing the appointment. My answers to the remaining questions are set out below.

## Q1 – DPO contact address and delivery metadata

I agree that retaining limited delivery metadata for the purpose of evidencing receipt and monitoring applicable response deadlines is appropriate.

However, I recommend excluding the subject line from the retained forwarding log. A subject line may itself contain substantive information, including potentially sensitive or special-category data, and should therefore not be treated as purely technical metadata.

The log should preferably be limited to:

- the date and time of receipt;
- the delivery status;
- a unique technical message identifier; and
- the sender's address only where necessary for managing the communication.

Access should remain restricted, and an appropriate retention period should be documented. Where a message constitutes a data-subject request, it can be entered separately into the Company's rights-request register and handled through the applicable internal procedure.

Subject to these adjustments, I have no objection to the proposed operational safeguard.

## Q2 – Local counsel and Route 2 admissions

I confirm that qualified local-counsel review is not intended to be a standing requirement for every Route 2 jurisdiction.

The relevant distinction is whether a material question of local law remains unresolved after completion of the admission screen and the applicable launch-day checks. Where such a material question remains, documented management risk acceptance cannot by itself replace the necessary legal clarification.

Accordingly, a separate US counsel opinion would not be required under this framework if:

- WI-1116 has been properly closed;
- the launch-day legislative checks have been completed and documented;
- all other conditions identified in the US screen have been closed; and
- no material unresolved question of US federal or state law remains.

This is a procedural conclusion concerning the admission framework. It should not be interpreted as substantive confirmation by me that the product complies with US federal or state law.

## Q3 – Commercial basis for EU AI Act advice

EU AI Act work is separate from the statutory DPO function and is not automatically included within the ordinary DPO retainer.

Future EU AI Act assignments may be separately requested and, following prior confirmation of the scope and estimated effort, billed at EUR 75 per hour. For clearly defined deliverables, we may alternatively agree on a fixed fee in advance.

The classification input contained in section 6 of my previous response may be treated as the initial classification advice connected with the present appointment process. Further work, including review of the completed classification record, Article 50 implementation, product changes or future reassessments, would be separately commissioned on the basis described above.

## Q4 – Teacher-reliance trigger

I agree with the substance of the proposed clarification. Mere incidental, learner-initiated use of AI-assisted material in ordinary schoolwork does not, by itself, bring the system within Annex III, point 3.

I suggest recording the trigger as follows:

> "Outputs are transferred to or relied upon by teachers or educational institutions to evaluate learning outcomes or determine grades, placement, promotion, certification, access to education or the direction of formal learning, where such reliance forms part of the system's intended purpose, is arranged, integrated, marketed or contracted by ZWIZZLY, or the system is otherwise used by or on behalf of an educational or vocational-training institution.
>
> Mere incidental, learner-initiated use of AI-assisted outputs in the learner's ordinary schoolwork does not by itself trigger reclassification. A reassessment is nevertheless required if the intended purpose, product functionality, marketing, contractual arrangements or actual systematic institutional use materially changes."

This preserves the present consumer-side use case while ensuring that systematic institutional adoption or a change in intended purpose triggers a new assessment.

## Q5 – Article 50(2) and the interactive chat

The persistent in-chat label and disclosure at the consent stage appear appropriate for Article 50(1), provided the disclosure is clear, accessible and present from the beginning of the interaction.

However, Article 50(1) and Article 50(2) impose separate obligations. I therefore cannot confirm a general limitation of Article 50(2) to exported or shareable content only. The fact that a user is visibly informed that they are interacting with an AI does not automatically remove the machine-readable marking and detectability requirement applicable to synthetic text outputs.

The implementation assessment should therefore cover both:

- exportable or shareable content, including recap documents; and
- synthetic text displayed in the interactive chat.

This does not necessarily mean that identical technical measures must be applied to both surfaces. The Company should first document:

- all synthetic-output surfaces;
- whether ZWIZZLY qualifies as the relevant provider for each surface;
- what marking or provenance functionality is supplied by the underlying model providers;
- the technical feasibility and current state of the art for chat-based text;
- the proposed machine-readable mechanism for each applicable surface; and
- any specific exclusion or proportionality argument relied upon.

Exportable content should be treated as an immediate and clearly applicable implementation item. The chat stream should remain within the assessment rather than being excluded in advance. A final technical position can then be documented by reference to the Commission's Article 50 Guidelines, the applicable Code of Practice and the system architecture.
